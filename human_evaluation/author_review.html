Sachit, we re-ran every scenario in your 200-question set with PHREEQC 3.8.6 and the same frozen phreeqc.dat used for the benchmark experiments, then compared the printed value for each question with its four options. This page lists what we found so you can fix the key. We have not changed the dataset.

For S01–S10 we used your original input files from LLM_Questions_SC (test1–10); they belong to an earlier option set but define the setup. test9 contains answer letters only, so S09 and all of S11–S20 were set up from the most literal reading of the question text with PHREEQC defaults. We never tuned an input to hit the key. Where nothing matched, we tried a few documented alternative readings (step numbering, units, charge balance). Redox-sensitive values were re-run with tighter or different KNOBS and kinetic-integrator settings; if the printed value changed at the digit that separates the options, the item is marked unstable.

Scenario-level issues

These affect every question in a scenario, including items whose key we could reproduce. A participant who follows the text literally can get different numbers.

  • S01 Calcite dissolution at 100°C. For Q5 and Q6 the key matches PHREEQC reaction step 48 rather than step 47, while Q7 matches step 47, so the step numbering in the key is inconsistent. pe is poorly poised in this system and moves by more than the option spacing under different kinetic integrator settings.
  • S05 Mineral–gas equilibrium. The text copies the GAS_PHASE keywords literally: "a 1 atm gas phase … CO2 at partial pressure 1 atm, CH4 at 5 atm, H2S at 10 atm" is physically contradictory (the partials sum to 16 atm) and the 1 L volume is not stated. The default PHREEQC reading happens to reproduce your input, but the earlier wording ("1:5:10 molar ratio") was clearer.
  • S06 Multi-mineral equilibrium. The text says "pure water solution at pH 3", but your input uses "pH 3 charge", which charge-balances the starting solution (to pH 6.119). A participant who sets a fixed pH 3 as written matches none of the keyed values. The text should say the pH is adjusted for charge balance.
  • S07 Barium solution and minerals. Same issue as S06: the text says "pH 5", your input uses "pH 5 charge" (starting pH 11.343). A fixed pH 5 as written matches none of the keyed values.
  • S09 CH₄ / CO₂ / NH₃ gas equilibrium. The text says "10 moles of Methane, 10 moles of CO2 and 5 moles of NH3", but the key is reproduced only when 10, 10 and 5 are entered as GAS_PHASE partial pressures (atm), which gives about 0.30, 0.30 and 0.15 mol. Truly 10/10/5 mol gives pH 6.991 and matches no option.
  • S12 River water–seawater mixing. "Cl 10" and "Cl 19000" have no units; the key is consistent with the default mmol/kgw. With no cations the mixed solution is almost entirely charge-imbalanced (19 mol/kgw Cl), so pe is undefined and the setup is not physically realistic.
  • S13 Stepwise sulfuric acid addition. pe and redox-derived values in acidified pure water have no redox couple to fix them, and change with solver settings.
  • S14 Gypsum equilibrium. Pure water with gypsum has no redox couple, so pe-derived values (pe, SI sulfur, H2) change with solver settings. Tightening the convergence tolerance moves pe from 10.741 to −0.920.
  • S15 CO₂ equilibrium. Pure water with CO2 has no redox couple, so pe-derived values change with solver settings.
  • S17 Ca–Sr carbonate solid solution. Ca 0.01, Sr 0.01 and alkalinity 0.04 have no units. With the PHREEQC default (mmol/kgw) no solid solution forms and nothing matches; with mol/kgw every non-redox key reproduces uniquely. The text should state mol/kgw.
